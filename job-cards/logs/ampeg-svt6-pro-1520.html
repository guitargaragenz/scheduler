Early August 2026 — light bulb limiter attempt, aborted

Tried to power up on a light bulb limiter. Stopped: bulb stayed at full brightness and the rails only came up to about half.

Not a fault — the limiter is too small. A 200 W bulb passes 0.87 A flat out at 230 V, against 33,600 µF of filter caps and a 1000 W transformer. From the card's own dim-bulb table, at 0.5 A drawn the amp only sees 132 V. That is the half rails, exactly.

Correcting the first read of it: the relays not pulling in doesn't make the bulb brighter — relays out means less load, not more. It's the other way round. The bulb is too small, so the rails sag, and below about 120 V the relays can never release — the +65 V rail scales with the mains and D11 is a 30 V zener that has to be passed first. Result is a locked stalemate that reads like a dead amp.

Decision: use the variac, not the bulb. This amp is a variac job.

Dating note: this happened at the bench in early August 2026, during the original diagnosis, and was only written up on 20 Sep. The 550 mA / half-rails figure in the card’s dim-bulb reference table is from this same event. It predates the repairs — the zeners, the four feed resistors, C14 and the recap all came after it.

Decisions taken

  • Card steps 9–12 skipped (bench-supply injection to prove the K1 / K2 relay chain). The variac ramp exercises the same chain in place, with a current limit and abort criteria. Accepted trade: if the relays don't click at 150–180 V, come back and do 9–12 to find out why.

2026-09-20 — trimmer direction traced off the print (desk work, amp not powered)

Anticlockwise is minimum bias on both AP1 and AP2. Traced at high magnification; both pots are wired identically.

  • The CW-marked leg goes to the bias transistor's base (Q2 for AP1, Q22 for AP2), with the 22 K collector-to-base above it.
  • The wiper is strapped back to the other end leg, which goes to common — and Trevor confirmed from the board that the emitter also goes to common. That last fact is what closed it.
  • So the pot sits base-to-emitter and the working resistance is CW leg → wiper. Classic Vbe multiplier: spread ≈ 0.65 × (1 + 22K / pot).
  • Fully anticlockwise = full 10 K in circuit = smallest spread = minimum bias.

Bench identification of the legs: two of the three read a dead short — wiper and common end. The odd leg out is the CW one, on the transistor base.

The travel is badly non-linear — 10 K ≈ 2.1 V spread, 5 K ≈ 3.5 V, 3 K ≈ 5.4 V, 1 K ≈ 15 V. Dead for the first half, then up fast, vicious at the end. Working range is around the middle-to-upper third.

2026-09-21 — pots wound to minimum, positions as found recorded (bench, amp dead — no power applied)

Both are single-turn trims (~270° of travel). Wound fully anticlockwise with the amp off. Travel from the as-found position to the anticlockwise end:

PotTravel to CCW endAs found, roughly
AP11/2 turn~2/3 of the way toward the CW end
AP21/4 turn~1/3 of the way toward the CW end

AP1's as-found position cross-checks the trace — the maths put the working point around the middle-to-upper third of the travel, and that's where it was sitting.

AP2 sat noticeably lower than AP1. Could be genuine device spread between the two halves, could be a previous hand. Don't expect the two pots to end up matched.

Do not wind them back to the as-found positions. Those belonged to the amp before this repair — different caps, different zeners, and C14 missing entirely, so B+ was wrong. Bias gets set by the meter, from minimum, every time. The numbers above are only useful for pacing the turns so you're not hunting blind.

2026-09-24 — no breaker bypass wire; card step 6 corrected

The mains wiring is entirely factory. Original PCB track, original breaker, no breaker→switch bypass wire — confirmed by Trevor at the bench.

Card step 6 had been written around a bypass wire that does not exist, and warned that C1/C2 (0.0033 μF suppression caps) might be left orphaned by it. They are not — they sit as designed and the EMI suppression is intact. Step 6 has been rewritten as a plain J25→J15 continuity check, which is still worth doing.

Where the bypass came from is unknown; it was not something Trevor did. Nothing else on the card depends on it.

2026-09-24 — board-out checks cleared, board closed up

Card steps 0.4 and 0.5 are both done and passed.

  • Step 0.4 — R50 (2K 2W) checked for drift, D14–D17 checked for shorts, F1 (1/8 A slow-blow in the B+ feed) checked. All good.
  • Step 0.5 — Q2 and Q22, the two MPS-A06 bias transistors, checked in circuit, not with a leg lifted. Both fine.

On the in-circuit test: the readings are pulled around by the 22K collector-to-base and the 10K trimpot sitting across base-emitter, so they won't look textbook. But a dead short collector-to-emitter — the failure that cooks the output stage — still shows up plainly in circuit, and that is ruled out. Mild leakage is the one thing in-circuit cannot clear, and it does not need a separate test: with both pots at minimum, a leaky bias transistor shows up on the ramp as bias that will not come down. Near zero millivolts at the 120 V pause clears it. 30 mV+ with the pots hard anticlockwise is the leaky transistor — variac straight down.

Nothing left needs the board open. Step 13's standoff work is already in place, so the amp can be reassembled for the variac ramp.

Open question before the ramp — the full recap versus card steps 0.1 and 0.2. Eight positions on this board must be non-polarised: C9, C10, C36, C38 (1 µF, film is fine) and C50, C51, C54, C55 (10 µF 35 V). If the recap put ordinary polarised electrolytics into any of them, they are being reverse-biased on half of every cycle. C10 matters most — it sits in series with R27 (2.2K) across the bias spreader, where there is no DC to define a polarity at all. Confirm what is actually fitted in those eight before ramping.

2026-09-24 — card tick-boxes do not persist to the file

The job card's checkboxes save to the browser's own local storage, not into the HTML file. Ticks made at the bench are invisible to anyone reading the file and are not captured by git — they live only on the machine and browser they were made on.

So this log is the file of record for what has been done, not the card's ticks. Use the card's Export notes button to lift the state out when it needs recording here.

2026-09-24 — the J31 fuse, and why step 24 is closed

The 4 A fuse at J31 is blown, and it is the original one. Trevor couldn’t recall whether he replaced it during the first round of testing. He didn’t — the reasoning is solid: J31 sits in the speaker output leg, and at the limiter attempt the amp only ever saw about 132 V with the relays never pulling in. No output current flowed at all, so nothing could have blown a fuse in that leg. The blown fuse in there now is the one the amp arrived with.

Card step 24 is closed. It asked why the previous tech fitted that fuse, on the theory that an earlier intermittent might be behind it. Pete has no history to give — he bought the amp second-hand in this condition. There is nothing to ask him.

The best available account stays the simple one: the fuse was fitted roughly 4× undersized, it blew on a loud night without anything failing downstream, and the amp went back to a customer still dead. An undersized fuse in a speaker line doesn’t need a fault to blow.

Before any cab goes on this amp, fit a fresh fuse at J31. For the variac ramp it doesn’t matter — no load, no output current — but card step 3 assumes a working 4 A is in there, and right now there isn’t.
