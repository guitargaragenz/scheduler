Standing hazards — read before every session

  • The output is not ground-referenced. This PCB is hardwired mono-bridge. Never put a grounded scope probe on either output leg — use differential, isolated or floating measurement.
  • Filter caps hold lethal charge long after power-down. Six 5600 µF/80 V plus two 47 µF/450 V (C14, C15) on a ~296 V B+. Discharge and verify 0 V before touching anything.
  • Mains-potential taps. The unused transformer primary tap sits at line potential when powered. It must be insulated, never landed on a tab.
A

Before power

power off
01

Turn AP1 and AP2 fully anticlockwise. That's minimum bias.

AP1 — topside, next to Q4 (+ half).
AP2 — underside, through the access hole (− half).
Both are 10K trimpots. Don't touch R26 or R100 — those are fixed resistors, not the pots.

The pots are touchy. The first half of the turn does almost nothing, then it comes up fast. You'll likely end up about two thirds of the way round. Turn in tiny bits and let the meter settle each time.

B

Variac ramp

no current limiting — hand on the dial
02

Know when to stop — read this before you start.

Variac straight down if:
  • Mains current keeps climbing while you hold the variac still.
  • Meter A (amps) or Meter B (millivolts) keeps creeping up on its own.
  • Meter B reads in volts instead of millivolts.
  • One rail meter (C or D) comes up and the other doesn't.
Always turn the variac down first, then touch the pots. Never the other way round.
03

Clip meters on before you touch the variac.

Meter A — + half (bank 1, hidden side). DC amps, in series with the J37 faston (+65 V feed to that side). Power off, unplug J37, meter on the 10 A range between the plug and the tab, both ends solid. Never turn its dial while it's in series — volts or ohms shorts the rail.
Meter B — − half (bank 2). DC millivolts across R141 (far-left white resistor). One probe each end, never to ground. 20 mV = 42 mA. Each half has its own bias pot, so each needs its own meter.
Meter C — +65 V rail at J38 to chassis ground. Not J37 — Meter A is in series there. DC volts. Tells you when the relay is about to click (150–180 V mains).
Meter D — −65 V rail to chassis ground (small Tenma). DC volts. Both rails should rise together — one lagging means a blown fuse or diode.
Mains current — read the variac's ammeter or a clamp meter. No DMM needed.

Use your best DMM on B — it's reading millivolts.

Never a scope across a source resistor. Its ground clip is earthed. A DMM is fine.

04

Bring the variac up in steps. Wait 30 s at each and write down the readings.

At 120 V, stop. Write down Meter A — that's the starting current for step 6. Meter B should read near zero. Meter B at 30 mV or more — variac down, the pots may be at the wrong end. If it's fine, give one pot a quarter turn to feel how much it moves, then put it back.

No click by 200 V — stop. Don't go on to 230 V. Variac down and find out why.

Bias

factory procedure, PCB 07-699-01
05

Set up: no load on the output, Tube Voltage pot at '300V', and do it straight after the ramp while the amp is still cool.

06

+ half (hidden side): turn AP1 slowly clockwise, watching Meter A, until it reads 0.21 A more than your starting current from the 120 V stop.

0.21 A = the five top FETs × 42 mA (20 mV across each 0.47 Ω). J37 only feeds the top five — the bottom five carry the same current in series. You can't reach the resistors on this side, so this total is the check — it can't spot one odd FET, but it shows runaway well.

08

Leave it idling 15–20 min, then check Meters A and B again.

Bias climbs as the heatsinks warm up — expect to turn it back down a little.

D

Before handover

09

Measure DC offset across the speaker output, no load, warm. A few tens of millivolts at most.

Meter across the two output legs — never a leg to ground. A big reading means the two halves don't match. Find that before a speaker does.

10

Quiet soak test, 1–2 hours. Only once step 9 passes.

Load: two 8 Ω 100 W resistors in parallel (= 4 Ω, 200 W). Both output leads to the load, neither to ground.
Fuse: fresh 4 A slow-blow in J31 (it blows around 64 W — keeps it gentle).
Signal: steady tone (100 Hz or 1 kHz), turned up until the load is warm.

Every 15–20 min: heatsinks warm not scorching, no smell, relays still in, current steady.
At the end: check bias again (Meters A and B), then a short, quiet listen on a speaker.

11

During the soak, check the ±16 V rail (J13 pin 2 and pin 3) still reads 15 V or more, and the stood-off zeners and resistors are secure.

If it's sagging below 15 V, the proper fix is a bigger regulator. Quote that separately — not part of this job.

12

Soak done — swap the J31 fuse for a 15 A slow-blow.

It protects the speaker cab, not the amp. 15 A decided 13 Oct — Pete won't get near full power.

13

Swap the mains breaker for an 8 A. It's currently 7 A.

8 A is the export spec. 7 A can trip at switch-on. Never go above 8 A — it protects the transformer.
