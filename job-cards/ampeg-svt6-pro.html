Standing hazards — read before every session

  • The output is not ground-referenced. This PCB is hardwired mono-bridge. Never put a grounded scope probe on either output leg — use differential, isolated or floating measurement.
  • Filter caps hold lethal charge long after power-down. Six 5600 µF/80 V plus three 47 µF/450 V on a ~296 V B+. Discharge and verify 0 V before touching anything.
  • A variac is an autotransformer, not an isolator. Always feed it from an isolation transformer.
  • Mains-potential taps. The unused transformer primary tap sits at line potential when powered. It must be insulated, never landed on a tab.
0

Board out — capacitor check

added Sat 7 Aug · do this first
0.1

Pull the polarised electrolytics out of C10, C50 and C54. These four positions — C9, C10 (1 µF) and C50, C54 (10 µF 35 V) — must be bipolar / non-polarised. Film is fine for the 1 µF parts.

Confirmed off the schematic. On this drawing a polarised cap is drawn with a + on one plate; a non-polarised one has no +. C9, C10, C50 and C54 have no +. C10 sits in series with R27 (2.2K) across the bias spreader — there is no DC across that pair to define a polarity, so a polarised part there is being reverse-biased on half of every cycle.

0.2

Check the minus-half twins the same way: C36, C38 (1 µF) and C51, C55 (10 µF 35 V). Also non-polarised.

Mirror positions in the other half of the bridge. Easy to miss because the fault half gets all the attention.

0.3

Everything else on the power amp and supply sheets is either film or genuinely polarised — leave it. Polarised (marked +): C3, C5, C11, C14, C15, C16, C17, C18, C19, C21, C22, C24, C25, C26, C29, C31, C32, C40, C44, C45, C46.

Film / non-polarised, no + on the print: C1, C2 (.0033 250V), C4, C6, C7, C8, C12, C13, C20, C23, C27, C28, C30, C33, C34, C35, C37, C39, C43, C47, C48, C49, C52, C53 — plus the four in step 0.1 and the four in step 0.2.

0.4

While the board is out: check R50 (2K 2W) for drift and D14–D17 (1N4007) for shorts.

These are the parts that take the hit if C14 ever shorted — which is the most likely reason it isn't fitted. R50 drifting high also pulls B+ down on its own.

0.5

Check the two bias transistors: Q2 (MPS-A06, works with trimpot AP1, plus half) and Q22 (MPS-A06, works with AP2, minus half). Diode-test each with one leg lifted — about 0.6 V base-to-emitter and base-to-collector one way, open the other way. Any reading collector-to-emitter means it's shorted — replace it.

These two set how hard the output FETs idle. Leaky or shorted and the bias runs away and cooks the output stage; open and you get crossover distortion. Same 2008 vintage as the zeners that failed, and they're cents each — if in any doubt just fit new ones. While you're there, sweep AP1 and AP2 end to end on the ohmmeter and watch for jumps or dead spots; a dropout on the wiper spikes bias.

A

Bench prep

power off throughout
01

Discharge the filter bank. Verify 0 V on ±65V, ±100V and B+ before touching anything.

02

Fit C14 (47 µF 450 V) — check polarity.

C14 is the first reservoir on the +296 V B+. C14 → R50 (2K 2W) → C15 is a two-stage filter for the tube plates; leaving C14 out roughly halves the smoothing, which shows up as hum and ghosting through the preamp. Most likely reason it's missing: it shorted, was removed and never replaced — so check R50 and D14–D17 before you fit a new one (step 0.4).

03

Leave the 4 A inline fuse at J31 in place for all bench work. Remove it and restore the stock connection only at final assembly, before handover.

J31 is the CH1(−) output leg — the fuse is in the speaker line, so it protects the cab, not the MOSFETs. It didn't save them; the 220 Ω feed resistors and the sagging rails did. At rated power that leg carries 12.2 A into 4 Ω (600 W) and 22.4 A into 2 Ω (1000 W), so 4 A opens at around 64 W — useless in service, but a good safety net on the bench.

At handover: pull it out (factory fitted none), or go up to 15 A slow-blow if you want one there. Anything smaller nuisance-blows on a loud note. If you want actual device protection, that belongs in the ±65 V rails — 10 A slow-blow per leg — not the output. Even then a fuse only limits collateral damage; silicon fails faster than any fuse can open.

04

Insulate the floating BLK/WHT transformer tap. Do not land it on a tab.

Seven primary leads = dual winding with taps for 100/120/220/230/240 V. Unused taps are meant to float. Connecting one shorts turns and destroys the transformer in seconds. Your dim-bulb result (half rails at 550 mA) already confirms the 230 V strapping is correct as it sits.

05

Confirm D20 / D23 (1N5353B 16 V) are the right way round — measured out of circuit.

A reversed zener looks like a short to your bench supply in stage B. Rule it out now rather than chasing a phantom later.

06

Buzz J25→J15 (expect ~0 Ω) and check which side of the breaker C1 taps.

You bypassed the board with a breaker→switch wire. Electrically identical, but if C1/C2 (0.0033 µF suppression caps) tap the post-breaker node, they're now orphaned — no fault, but you lose EMI suppression.

07

Set AP1 and AP2 to minimum bias.

AP1 (R26) is topside next to Q4. AP2 (R100) is on the underside, adjusted through the access hole. Twenty IRFP240/9240 at an unknown idle current is the one load in this amp that can run away — positive tempco at low idle. Establish trimmer direction at low mains where being wrong is survivable.

B

Relay chain test

bench supplies — no mains
08

Lift all four feed resistors (220 Ω 10 W) so you don't backfeed the ±65 V rails: R21 / R63 on the negative side and R51 / R64 on the positive side.

There are two separate shunt chains, one per rail. Lifting only R21/R63 leaves the +16 V side still fed from +65 V.

09

Inject ±15.5 V at the ±16 V rail, ~100 mA limit. Not 16.0 V. Load it properly — preamp connected, both channels up, fans running — and read the supply's current meter. Target ~50 mA.

1N5353B is 16 V ±5% — a low-tolerance part starts conducting hard at 16.0 and eats your current limit. 15.5 V is comfortably inside TL074 spec and keeps the zeners off, so you read pure load.

Pass / fail: 40–60 mA = normal, change no values. Under ~90 mA = acceptable, rail stays regulated. Over ~90 mA = the rail will sag; needs a TO-220 pass transistor off the zener as a reference — separate quote.

The chain is 65 V → R21 → R63 (440 Ω series) → ~17 V node, so only ~110 mA total is available. The zener dumps whatever the load doesn't take, so it runs hottest at lightest load (1.8 W). That's the browning — a cooling problem, not a current problem.

10

Feed +60 V to the relay supply.

11

Satisfy the UVLO: two 30 V supplies in series → 60 V into the +65 V rail, or ~60 V straight onto C11.

D11 is a 1N4751, 30 V. A single 30 V supply sits right on its knee and won't release — you'd get a false "relay chain dead". C11 is a 63 V part, so keep it at 60, not higher. With ±100 V absent the VAS is dead and draw is negligible.

12

K1 / K2 should pull in. If not, scope IC1-D, then the over-temp path (IC1-C, TS1/TS2 LM35DZ on J44/J45).

This is the pass/fail on everything you've repaired. Two independent inhibits hold the relays: under-voltage and over-temp. At room temp the over-temp path should be permissive.

13

Power down. Refit all four stock 220 Ω resistors — R21 / R63 and R51 / R64 — standing 3–5 mm off the board. Stand D20 / D23 off too, with high-temp fibreglass sleeving on their legs.

Don't change the values — at ~110 mA the resistors only see 2.7 W each in 10 W parts, and there's no headroom to raise the resistance anyway. The fix is air. Axial zeners shed heat through their leads, so a nominal 5 W part in free air really handles 1–3 W. 3–5 mm only: longer legs work the joints loose in a road amp. Dab of neutral-cure (sensor-safe) silicone to steady them.

C

Variac ramp

no current limiting — hand on the dial
14

Isolation transformer → variac → amp. Never variac alone.

15

Clip meters on before you dial anything up: one source resistor per bank, one main rail, ammeter in line where you can see it without looking away from the variac.

16

Ramp in steps, pause 30 s at each and log the reading.

Ramp log
MainsExpectedActual IRail VObservations
30–50 V Tens of mA. Over ~200 mA — stop. That's a short.
50–100 V Gentle rise. Tube heaters warming.
150–180 V Relays should click in. D11 is a 30 V zener — UVLO releases here.
230 V Idle roughly 0.5–1 A once biased.

Relays click in at 150–180 V on the variac — and give it 1–3 seconds after you get there before you call it dead. C11 is 220 µF and has to charge first, so the click is delayed, not instant. Below about 120 V it will never release: the +65 V rail scales with the mains, and D11 is a 30 V zener that has to be passed before the circuit lets go.

If they pull in at 150–180 V, the ±16 V rail, IC1, the under-voltage chain and the relay drivers are all good. If they haven't clicked by 200 V, stop there — don't push on to 230. Go back to stage B and find out why.

17

Abort criteria — know these before you start.

Kill it immediately if either occurs:
  • Current climbing while voltage is held constant — that's thermal runaway building.
  • Source-resistor millivolts drifting upward on their own.
Variac down first. Trimmer second. Never the other way round.

21

Run 15–20 min, then re-check both banks.

IRFP240/9240 are positive-tempco at low idle. Bias will climb as the sinks warm — expect to pull it back. A single resistor is a proxy, not the spec; the spec is the average of ten.

E

Before handover

22

Fit the correct 8 A breaker. Currently 7 A.

Print calls out 15 A domestic/Japan, 8 A export. 7 A is undersized 12.5% — the safe direction, but marginal on inrush (six 5600 µF plus a large EI transformer) and at sustained high output. Never go above 8 A — that's the transformer's protection.

23

Confirm the ±16 V rail still holds ≥15 V under full load, and that the standoff work from step 13 is secure.

The resistors are fine as stock — don't uprate them. The heat is the zeners: they dump whatever the load leaves over, up to 1.8 W, through leads only. Standing them off is the repair. If the measured load came in over ~90 mA, the rail will sag and the real fix is a TO-220 series-pass transistor using the zener as its reference — quote that separately, don't do it inside this job.

23b

Measure DC offset across the speaker output, no load, warm. Should be a few tens of millivolts at most.

Measure across the two output legs, not from either leg to chassis ground. The output is bridged and neither side sits at ground. A big offset here means one half is biased differently from the other — find that before a speaker does.

24

Ask the customer why the previous tech fitted that fuse.

Someone added it deliberately, which usually means they were chasing something. Earlier repair history may point at an intermittent that predates all of this.
